Nasdaq: NTRB · Orlando, Florida

Abuse-deterrent technology for transdermal patches.

Nutriband is developing a portfolio of transdermal pharmaceutical products. Its lead product under development, AVERSA Fentanyl, combines an approved generic fentanyl patch with the company's AVERSA abuse-deterrent technology.

Investigational AVERSA Fentanyl is not approved by the FDA, and its abuse-deterrent properties have yet to be demonstrated in a human abuse potential study. Fiscal year ends January 31.

01 The problem

A fentanyl patch is designed to deliver drug over three days. That payload is the target.

Transdermal versions of opioid and stimulant drugs offer improved pharmacokinetic delivery and patient convenience, with wear times of up to seven days. They also carry an increased drug payload, which the 10-K says can be a target for recreational drug abusers or subject to accidental pediatric exposure.

According to the FDA, as the 10-K cites it, young children have died or become seriously ill after exposure to a skin patch containing fentanyl, and children can overdose on new and used patches by putting them in their mouths or sticking them on their skin.

The company says the oral, buccal and inhaled routes its technology targets represent as much as 70% of all transdermal fentanyl abuse. That 70% is the company's own statement; the figures beside this text are the poison-center data.

02 How AVERSA is designed to work

Aversive agents sit on the backing, apart from the drug.

The company coats taste and sensory aversive agents onto the backing of an approved patch. The design is intended to make abuse by mouth an unpleasant experience. The filings describe an IND filing for a human abuse potential study as the next step; none is reported yet.

  1. 1 Aversive-agent coating. Taste and sensory agents coated onto the backing in a controlled-release formulation.
  2. 2 Drug matrix. The approved generic fentanyl patch. The company's plan makes no change to the drug matrix, its demonstrated safety or its release characteristics.
  3. 3 Skin, or the mouth. In A, the plan is for drug delivery to continue as the approved patch does it, and the 10-K says the aversive agents are not delivered to the skin during wear. In B, the dashed outline marks the mouth, the route where the agents are intended to make abuse very unpleasant.

Schematic, not to scale, drawn from the company's description in its 10-K. The 10-K says the agents are also available after the patch is used and are hard to separate from the drug by extraction. The company has not reported results from a human abuse potential study.

The design in three verbs

  1. 01

    Coat

    Aversive agents are coated onto the backing of the patch, not mixed into the drug matrix.

  2. 02

    Separate

    Physical separation from the drug is designed to leave the approved patch's matrix, safety and release characteristics as they are.

  3. 03

    Deter

    If the patch is misused by the oral, buccal or inhaled routes, the agents are intended to make that a very unpleasant experience.

05 Material disclosures

In the company's own filed words.

These are the points in the filings that most affect how the figures above should be read. Quotation marks mark text copied from the filing; each item links to its source.

  1. 01

    Funding need

    "We will require approximately $13 million for research and development of our abuse deterrent fentanyl transdermal system, including clinical manufacturing and clinical trials that need to be completed in order to obtain FDA approval. However, the total cost could be substantially in excess of that amount." Cash was $3,421,615 on Jul 31, 2026.

    10-K, Item 710-Q (cash)
  2. 02

    Going concern assessment

    "The Company has generated operating losses since its inception and has relied on sales of securities and the issuance of third-party and related-party debt to support cash flow from operations." Management concluded: "Management believes the substantial doubt about the ability of the Company to continue as a going concern is alleviated by the above assessment." The latest 10-Q also warns: "If we cannot achieve profitability, we may be forced to cease operations and you may suffer a total loss of your investment."

    10-K, Item 710-Q, Part II, Item 1A
  3. 03

    Revenue concentration and decline

    "For the year ended January 31, 2026, three customers accounted for 63% of total revenue. The Company lost a customer that represented 24% of total revenue." In the quarter ended July 31, 2026 revenue was $437,514, against $622,452: "one of Company's principal customers moved its operations to Asia. A decrease in demand is expected in the balance of the current year."

    10-K, Item 8 (notes)10-Q, Item 2
  4. 04

    Pocono sale agreement terminated

    A December 2025 agreement to sell a 90% interest in Pocono Pharmaceuticals to Earth Vision Bio Inc., at a total purchase price of $5 million, was terminated on February 13, 2026, "due to the purchaser's failure to pay applicable late fees" after it missed the December 31, 2025 closing date. The company received $30,000 in late fees and no further payments after January 21, 2026.

    8-K, Feb 13, 20268-K, Dec 30, 202510-K, Item 8 (notes)
  5. 05

    Controls and procedures

    Management says it "concluded that our disclosure controls and procedures are not effective in ensuring that information required to be disclosed by us in the reports that we file or submit under the Securities Exchange Act is recorded, processed, summarized and reported, within the time periods specified in the SEC's rules and forms." Management reports material weaknesses "due to the absence of segregation of duties, as well as lack of qualified accounting personnel, and excessive reliance on third-party consultants for accounting, financial reporting and related activities," and says it has since added qualified accounting personnel and monitoring controls.

    10-Q, Item 4
  6. 06

    Litigation

    Joseph Gunnar, LLC and Lucosky Brookman LLP have sued in New York state court over the company's termination of an engagement letter for a public offering; Gunnar seeks over $500,000 plus punitive damages and Lucosky Brookman seeks reimbursement of legal fees. The company denies the claims and has counterclaimed, seeking $1,000,000 on each counterclaim. The case is in discovery.

    10-Q, Part II
  7. 07

    Warrants were due to expire September 30, 2026

    The publicly traded warrants (Nasdaq: NTRBW) were due to expire at 5:00 p.m. Eastern Time on September 30, 2026. Per the 8-K, trading was to cease at the close that day and unexercised warrants were to be voided, with no impact on the common stock. The filings reviewed for this page do not report the outcome.

    8-K, Sep 23, 2026
  8. 08

    Capital structure and related parties

    3,008,643 shares of non-voting Series A convertible preferred stock are outstanding; each becomes convertible into one common share only after FDA approval for commercial sale of an AVERSA-based product. A three-year $5,000,000 credit line facility, at 7% and not convertible into common stock, was amended on June 1, 2026, replacing the March 2023 facility. That earlier facility was with a related party, TII Jet Services LDA, per the 10-K; the 8-K does not name the lender. The 4P Therapeutics purchase agreement also requires a 6% royalty to its former owner on revenue from the acquired abuse-deterrent intellectual property.
